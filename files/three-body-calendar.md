# The Three-Body Calendar

*A calendar in which every boundary is an observable sky event. Nothing is decreed; everything can be checked by looking up.*

Every rhythm in this calendar arises from the mutual gravitation of three masses — Earth, Moon, and Sun. The day is Earth's spin; the month is the Moon's orbit; the year is Earth's orbit; the nineteen-year rhyme is the tension between the last two; and the ages are opened by Earth's own shadow falling on the Moon, its timing set by the Sun's torque on the Moon's tilted orbit. There is no fourth wheel. The system is quasi-periodic and precisely predictable, yet its frequencies share no common period, so the calendar rhymes endlessly and never repeats.

---

## Part I — The Constitution

*These are the rules. There are seven tiers, ordered by scale. Each is governed by one body or one relationship between bodies.*

**Day** — governed by Earth's spin. Runs midnight to midnight, civil local time (chosen for interoperability with the civil world; a sunset-to-sunset variant is equally valid and more traditional — this is the one open choice in the constitution).

**Lunar quarter** — governed by the moon. Four per moon, opening at the four visible phase gates: full moon, waning half, dark moon, waxing half. Named *waning bright, waning dark, waxing dark, waxing bright*. Roughly 7.4 days each: the sky-true week.

**Moon** — governed by the moon. Runs full moon to full moon, 29 or 30 days, never cut, never stretched. Numbered within the solar year by its opening: Moon 1 is the first full moon on or after the winter solstice. A year contains 12 or 13 moon-openings.

**Solar quarter** — governed by the sun. Four per year, opening at the exact instants of the winter solstice, spring equinox, summer solstice, autumn equinox. Unequal by nature (roughly 89 to 94 days — the signature of Earth's elliptical orbit). These are boundaries of light, not of warmth; the felt seasons lag them by about a month and belong to climate, not to the calendar.

**Year** — governed by the sun. Runs winter solstice to winter solstice, exactly, always. Labeled by the Gregorian year it mostly occupies.

**Cycle (the rhyme)** — governed by the sun and moon together. Nineteen years, rigid, no recalibration. The year's place in the cycle (1–19) is its Golden Number; the year's *epact* is the **signed** offset (about −15 to +15 days) from the solstice to the nearest year-opening full moon — negative when the full moon precedes the solstice. It steps by about 11 days per year and passes through zero once per cycle; the year whose epact is nearest zero is Year 1. (A signed epact is required: the Age of 2010 opened with its full moon ~15 h *before* the solstice, which an unsigned 0–29 count would misreport as 29 rather than −0.6.) Cycle counting restarts at each age opening.

**Age** — governed by sun, moon, and shadow together. An age opens at the **first total lunar eclipse falling within one day of a winter-solstice instant** in a new eclipse chain. Later qualifying eclipses of the same chain (19 years on) are **confirmation nights**, not turnovers. Ages are not numbered. They are named by witnesses from the opening night alone: the **house** (the constellation hosting the eclipsed moon) and the **given name** (the witnessed color of totality — Ash, Umber, Rust, Brick, or Copper, after the Danjon scale), with epithets from the night's seal. The name records what the sky did, never what anyone hopes.

**Which body governs each tier.** Day — Earth's spin. Lunar quarter and moon — the Moon's orbit. Solar quarter and year — Earth's orbit. Cycle — Moon and Earth-orbit in tension. Age — Earth's shadow, timed by the Sun's torque on the Moon's nodes. Earth is the silent partner throughout: we stand on the body whose spin, orbit, orbital plane, and shadow define four of the seven tiers.

**The federation principle.** The sun and moon tracks are sovereign and independent. Moons straddle quarter boundaries freely; the year never waits for the moon; the moon is never cut by the year. Every day therefore carries a dual address, like "Tuesday the 4th" — one coordinate from each track, related but unsubordinated.

## Part II — The Address

Any moment is fully located as:

> **Age · Cycle C, Year Y of 19 · [Quarter], day q · Moon M, day d · [lunar quarter], day n · (Gregorian date)**

Example, 4 August 2026:

> **Age of 2010 · Cycle 1, Year 16 · Summer, day 45 · Moon 8, day 6 · waning bright, day 6 · (4 Aug 2026)**

Every element is verifiable by observation: the quarter by the sun's height, the moon-day by tonight's phase, the age by living memory of its opening eclipse.

## Part IV — The Commentary (the engine room)

*None of this is rules. It is why the rules produce what they produce. It can be ignored in daily life.*

**Why the epact steps by 11.** Twelve lunations run ~11 days short of the solar year, so Moon 1's arrival slides ~11 days earlier each year, wrapping every 19 years — the Metonic rhyme (235 lunations ≈ 19 years, residue ~2 hours).

**Why eclipse chains run four beats.** The Metonic return holds a full moon steady against the solstice (2-hour drift per beat), but 19 years contain 20.021 eclipse years, so the node slips **7.3 days per beat**. The eclipse window is ~22 days wide: four beats and the shadow has crossed it. The four beats — approach (partial), heart (total, opens the age), departure (total, confirms), expiry (penumbral) — are contributed by four different Saros families, ten numbers apart.

**Why the closing stubs are 8, 11, or 3.** Years 19 apart form a "lane"; nineteen lanes tile the lunation at 1.554-day spacing, and a taut lane's nearest taut neighbors sit **8 years** (moon ~1.6 d late) and **11 years** (~1.5 d early) away — the two sub-resonances from which 19 = 8 + 11 is built. A new age ignites on a neighboring lane, and the stub is the hop: 8 or 11 for single hops, compounds mod 19 (e.g. 8+8 → 3) when slack centuries pass between.

**Why taut and slack eras alternate.** The crown of tautness migrates one lane per ~**334 years** (4131 lunations — the next rung of the 8/11/19 continued-fraction ladder). Eras where the crown lane sits within hours of the solstice yield quick pairs of ages (65–84 years apart, one per node); slack eras yield the long silent ages (~307 years).

**The grand cycles.** **307 years** (3797 lunations ≈ 323.5 eclipse years) returns the solstice syzygy at the *opposite* node; **372 years** (4601 lunations ≈ 392 eclipse years) returns it at the *same* node. The age lineage is these two interleaved: 1247+372=1619, 1703+372=2075, 2010+372=2382; 1703+307=2010, 2075+307=2382.

**The spiral.** No reset ever occurs. The 372-year return leaves residues — ~3.4 h in phase, ~4.5 d at the node, ~1 d in the moon's ellipse, 5.2° of precession — and the four frequencies (synodic month, tropical year, eclipse year, anomalistic month) share no common period at all. All four are products of the same three-body system: the eclipse year comes from the Sun's torque regressing the Moon's nodes every 18.6 years, the anomalistic month from its apsidal precession every 8.85. The lunar three-body problem has no closed-form general solution — though this particular system is quasi-periodic and predictable, which is why five millennia of eclipses can be tabulated at all. Its frequencies are simply mutually irrational. Every recurrence is therefore a rhyme, never a repetition; every age's seal is unique forever. The calendar is a spiral transcribed, not a circle enforced — a readout of the three-body problem itself.

**Maintenance.** Nothing physical can drift: months, quarters, and years are observational. Only the cycle label ages, at ~2 h per beat — harmless for ~1,200 years — and every age opening flushes it. The system is self-correcting on the timescale of its own ceremonies.

## Part V — Open Items

1. **Unverified lineage entries: 1247 and 2382.** Check each against the NASA canon's century page before treating them as ages. The working model's *timings* matched the canon within minutes throughout, but its *type* classification failed twice at the total/partial boundary (it wrongly promoted the magnitude-0.90 partials of 1619 and 2075). Rule for the implementation: compute dates and gaps locally; take eclipse type from the canon.
2. **Day boundary** — civil midnight (current choice, chosen for interoperability) vs sunset. The one genuinely open design decision.
3. **Build state.** Reference implementation: `calendar-mechanism.html` — gear train, seven-tier ledger, syzygy scanner, all astronomy computed live from Meeus series with no dependencies. Not yet built: the two-track month grid (moons as the grid, solar quarters as cell tint, dual Gregorian dates), and the Google Calendar layer.
4. **Beta plan.** Ship as a PWA first: no server, no accounts, everything computed on-device, installable to the home screen and fully offline. Google sync via browser PKCE (Google Identity Services + Calendar REST API called directly from the page, tokens never leaving the device), events cached in IndexedDB. Expect the "unverified app" consent screen while in testing; Google verification is only needed to go public with the sensitive scope. Start read-only — render existing events inside the lunar grid — before attempting event creation or two-way sync.
5. **Scope discipline for the beta.** Ship the daily layer only: today's dual address, the month grid, phases and stations. Cycles, ages, seals, and the syzygy scanner belong behind a "deep time" page or a later version.
